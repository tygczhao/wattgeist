# WattGeist product

WattGeist brings back the home energy dashboards that Google PowerMeter (2009–2011) and Microsoft Hohm (2009–2012)
tried to offer. Both died because they depended on utility partnerships for data. WattGeist works from the file the
user can already download from their supplier or meter portal.

## Who it is for

- A household that has downloaded a smart meter export and wants to know where the money goes.
- The first question is almost always "what is my house burning while we sleep?" (the always-on, or vampire, load).

## The four features

1. **Meter import**: any CSV, XLSX or Green Button XML export, read correctly without the user mapping columns.
2. **Vampire hunt**: the always-on floor in watts and its yearly cost, haunted nights, and a suspect-device checklist
   using NRDC's measured standby watts.
3. **Tariff simulator**: the last 12 months of real usage billed on flat, time-of-use and half-hourly tariffs.
4. **Séance assistant**: questions in plain language, answered only from tool results.

## Voice

- Halloween is the costume, not the content. One spooky touch per screen, never in a number or a warning.
- Every figure says what it is: W for power, kWh for energy, currency with two decimals.
- Estimates are labelled as estimates (single-rate cost of the floor, load shifting, the London comparison).

## Out of scope

- Controlling devices, switching suppliers, or storing anyone's data outside their own machine.
